SpaceX (Space Exploration Technologies Corp.) is a private American aerospace manufacturer and space transportation company founded in 2002 by Elon Musk with the goal of reducing space transportation costs and enabling the colonization of Mars. Headquartered in Hawthorne, California, SpaceX designs, manufactures, and launches advanced rockets and spacecraft, most notably the Falcon 1 (now retired), Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, and the fully reusable Starship system. Falcon 9 is a two-stage orbital rocket known for its first-stage reusability, routinely landing boosters on drone ships or landing zones, while Falcon Heavy is one of the most powerful operational rockets, capable of carrying large payloads to orbit and deep space. SpaceX’s Dragon spacecraft family includes Cargo Dragon, which delivers supplies to the International Space Station (ISS), and Crew Dragon, which transports astronauts for NASA under the Commercial Crew Program, making SpaceX the first private company to send humans to orbit. The company has completed numerous missions including ISS resupply, crewed missions, satellite launches, interplanetary payloads, and national security missions. A major project is Starlink, a rapidly expanding constellation of thousands of low-Earth-orbit satellites providing global broadband internet. SpaceX’s long-term focus is Starship, a fully reusable, super heavy-lift launch system designed for missions to the Moon under NASA’s Artemis program and eventually to Mars. Through rapid innovation, vertical integration, reusable launch technology, and frequent launches, SpaceX has become a dominant force in the global space industry, significantly reshaping how space missions are conducted.
